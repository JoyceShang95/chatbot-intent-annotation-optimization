Business problem

Improve customer-service chatbot intent recognition so consumer questions reach the correct workflow or knowledge-base answer.

10 intents

order_tracking delivery_delay address_change order_cancellation refund_status return_request payment_failed duplicate_charge wrong_item damaged_item

Key insight

The difficult cases are not obvious requests; they are boundary cases where one message contains signals for two intents.

“Where is my package? It should have arrived yesterday.”

Correct intent: delivery_delay
Temporal overdue signal should outweigh generic tracking wording.

Confusion hotspots

Tracking ↔ Delay

Model overweights “track / where is / package” and misses overdue signals.

Return ↔ Refund

Model stops on “return” without identifying the requested outcome.

Payment Failed ↔ Duplicate Charge

Pending authorization can look like a duplicate charge without context.

Optimization actions

  1. Add hard-negative pairs to the training set.
  2. Refine annotation rules around temporal signals and requested outcome.
  3. Flag low-confidence samples for human review.
  4. Turn recurring gaps into new knowledge-base examples.
  5. Track per-intent precision/recall and confusion trends over time.

Knowledge-base gap

Generic tracking instructions are not enough for messages that indicate an overdue shipment.

New rule: If the promised date has passed, route to the delivery-delay flow first.

Suggested FAQ

My order is late. What should I do?

Check the promised delivery date and latest carrier scan. If the promised date has passed, classify the issue as delivery_delay before offering generic tracking instructions.
